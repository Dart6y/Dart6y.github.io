01 / Current research

When a passing check isn't enough.

Investigating the gap between what security requirements say and what automated checks actually verify.

◉ Independent research · ongoing

Semantic Fidelity of Automated Security and Compliance Checks

A security check may execute exactly as written while testing something different from the requirement it claims to verify. This research examines that semantic mismatch, the risk of false assurance, and ways to evaluate whether automated checks faithfully represent their intended controls.

The work is ongoing. Current questions include the role of machine learning in interpreting natural-language requirements, and how ML-assisted verification itself should be independently tested. No completed validator or peer-reviewed publication is claimed.

Security assuranceGRC automationSemantic fidelityTrustworthy AI

“An automated security check could do precisely what its code instructed and still fail to do what the security requirement demanded.”

Research motivation: understand false PASS outcomes before organisations place confidence in an inaccurate automated assessment.

Status: research in progress

PROJECT / 01Radio sensing + ML
Final-year research · 2026

Camera-free indoor sensing with Wi-Fi

Designed a controlled indoor presence and coarse-location system using six ESP32 nodes, directed RSSI links, radio tomographic imaging and Random Forest models. Tested generalisation with session-aware evaluation rather than relying on inflated random-window results.

98.88%Presence accuracy on a session-aware held-out evaluation
Localisation exact-cell accuracy: 66.30%. These figures describe a controlled 2 × 2 m, 16-cell experiment and are not general deployment claims.

03 / Working principles

How I think about reliable systems.

Research becomes useful when we question assumptions, measure failure and make limitations explicit.

01 / TEST THE TEST

Check what a result actually means.

A successful execution is not necessarily evidence that the intended requirement was verified.

02 / EVALUATE HONESTLY

Look beyond impressive metrics.

Data leakage, shifts and adversarial inputs can reveal weaknesses that a convenient split obscures.

03 / SHOW YOUR LIMITS

Make conclusions reproducible.

Describe the scope of the experiment, separate measured results from hypotheses and document uncertainty.
